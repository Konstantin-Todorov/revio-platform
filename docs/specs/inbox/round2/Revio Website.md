# **Revio — The Perfect Pitch (Strategy + Website)**

Built through four lenses at once: a **software+hospitality copywriter** (AIDA), a **hospitality marketing/brand director**, a **tech sales director**, and a **hotel manager** who lives the pain. Analysis first, then the copy that flows from it. Same site structure and design as today — this is the pitch, not a rebuild.

**Two audiences, one page.** Hotels must feel *"they speak my language."* Investors must feel *"they understand this business and built a structural advantage."* Every section below is written so the same words satisfy both. And the brand reads **modern, tech-savvy, solutions-oriented, easy to work with** — never small.

## **1 · The pitch in one breath**

**For a hotelier:** Revio runs your channels, your reservations, your front desk and your own booking page off *one shared set of rooms* — so they can never fall out of sync, and you can start with the one product that fixes today's problem and add the rest with a switch, not a migration.

**For an investor:** Revio is a **composable hotel-commerce platform** with a structural moat — one inventory core instead of synced copies — and a **land-and-expand** model (enter on one product, expand across four) in a large, fragmented, migration-locked market where even the modern leaders still bolt distribution onto a third-party sync.

## **2 · What Revio actually is**

Four products on **one core**:

**RevioLink** — channel manager (distribution)

**RevioCRS** — central reservations + analytics

**RevioPMS** — property operations

**RevioDirect** — commission-light booking engine

The core is the product. Your rooms, rates and guests are **one record every product reads and writes** — not copies an integration keeps in step. From that single fact flow: no overbooking (nothing to drift), no migration (the next product already holds your data), trustworthy numbers (computed from one source), and one price from search to folio. Under the hood: database-level tenant isolation, real OTA connectivity via Channex, and occupancy-based pricing across the whole suite.

**The commercial engine (investor read):** land on the customer's loudest pain with a single product at a low, transparent entry price; expand across the suite with *zero migration friction* → high net revenue retention, cheap expansion, and a moat that compounds as more of the property runs on the shared core.

## **3 · Compset benchmark — where Revio sits**

The market forces a compromise. Four archetypes, each with a structural catch:

| Archetype (examples) | What it's good at | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel manager only (SiteMinder-class) | Distribution, at scale | Stops at distribution — you bolt on a separate PMS/CRS and pay to keep them in step |
| All-in-one suite (Cloudbeds, RoomRaccoon, Little Hotelier) | Turnkey, bundled, one login | All-or-nothing: the migration is the purchase, and it's self-service to configure |
| Open / API platform (Mews, Apaleo) | Modern, flexible, big marketplace | "Build-it-yourself" — real flexibility needs a developer/tech partner; modules and marketplace apps add up; distribution often routes through a third-party channel manager (an extra hop that lags) |
| Enterprise (Oracle OPERA) | Depth, scale | Complex; needs a dedicated IT team |

**The white space Revio owns:** *composable****and****turnkey, on one core, priced transparently — built for hoteliers, not developers.* Apaleo is composable **for developers**; Revio is composable **for a hotel manager.** All-in-ones give simplicity but trap you in a migration; open platforms give flexibility but demand a dev. Revio is the one design that asks for neither compromise — and because distribution *is* the core, there's no third-party sync hop to drift.

**Real-world proof the thesis is right (from the market itself):** hotels routinely lose nights to rates that lag on one channel because distribution is a polling "integration," not one source of truth; the fix — a channel-manager migration — commonly runs weeks and thousands in lost inventory. Revio removes the category of problem *and* the category of migration.

## **4 · SWOT (honest — this is what makes it investor-credible)**

**Strengths**

Architectural moat: one core, no synced copies → "consequences, not features" a competitor can't ship in a release.

Composable land-and-expand with **zero-migration** expansion — rare in the category.

Transparent, published pricing vs. hidden module/marketplace fee stacks.

Direct-booking channel at **2%** on the same core.

**Proof-led:** everything is demonstrable in a live product today.

Hotelier-fluent, done-for-you onboarding; EU invoicing/fiscalization depth.

**Weaknesses**

Young brand, low awareness vs. entrenched incumbents.

Smaller integration marketplace than the API-first players.

No enterprise credentials yet (SOC 2 / ISO / contractual SLA).

Unproven across many properties and consecutive high seasons *(address by evidence and risk-reversal, never by apologising for size).*

**Opportunities**

A large, fragmented independent/small-group segment frustrated by migration lock-in and OTA commission.

The unclaimed "composable-for-hoteliers" position.

**AI-readiness:** one clean commerce core is the ideal substrate for AI agents — most hotel systems aren't built for this yet; Revio's architecture is a natural fit as this becomes a buying criterion.

Regional/EU depth (fiscalization, VAT) most global players treat as an afterthought.

**Threats**

Incumbents bolting on "composability" or better sync.

A modern player moving down-market into Revio's segment.

Switching inertia and long consideration cycles.

Distribution-landscape shifts (Google, OTA terms).

**The strategic read:** Revio's edge is *structural and defensible*; its gaps are *stage-related and closeable*. The site's job is to make the structural edge unmissable and neutralise the stage gaps with proof and risk-reversal — never with "we're small."

**Can't oversell** → *reliability.* One availability number; no third-party sync hop to drift.

## **6 · Brand & voice**

**Attributes to project:** modern · tech-savvy (explained simply) · solutions-oriented · easy to work with · fluent in hotels. **Never:** small, scrappy, "for a company our size."

**Voice rules:** lead with the outcome a hotelier recognises; demote the mechanism to a one-line proof; use their words (*ADR, RevPAR, pickup, stop-sell, the last room, night audit*), not ours (*ARI loop, PWA, minor units*). Confident, plain, and specific — sophistication shown through clarity, not jargon.

**Investor-credibility layer (baked into the copy, not bolted on):** category clarity, commercial fluency (commission math, occupancy, parity), an explicit moat, the land-and-expand motion, and visible production-readiness.
