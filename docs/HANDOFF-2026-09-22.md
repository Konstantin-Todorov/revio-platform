# What I could not settle — a hand-off, 2026-09-22

Written for a model with more room to think than I had, and for whoever reads this after it.

**The rule of this file: an item is here because I am genuinely unsure, or because settling it
needs reasoning across more of the system than one change should carry — not because it is large.**
Large-and-clear work belongs on the roadmap at `operator.reviosoft.app/platform-history`. Things
waiting on a human belong in `ACTION-REQUIRED.md`. What is *true right now* belongs in `STATUS.md`.
This file is the third thing: known unknowns.

Each item says **what I believe**, **how confident I am and why**, and **what would settle it**.
Where I am guessing, it says so. Anything I could have checked and simply did not is marked
`NOT CHECKED` rather than dressed up as an opinion.

---

## 1. The job retry policy contradicts itself, and I did not resolve it deliberately

**What I believe.** Thirteen job routes hold a lease. Two (`auto-assign`, `auto-close-day`) release
it on failure through `withJobLease`, so the next tick retries. Five state in their own comments
that a failed run should *wait out its TTL* rather than retry. Both positions are defensible; the
codebase holding both is not.

**Confidence: high on the facts, none on the answer.** I read all thirteen. The decision is a
product judgement about what a duplicate run costs versus what a delayed one costs, and it differs
per job — a repeated hold-expiry is harmless, a repeated night audit rolls the business date twice.

**Why it is harder than it looks.** It is not one decision, it is four interacting ones:

1. the per-route policy above,
2. `run-jobs.mjs` has no retry at all — and **must not get a blanket one**, because a job that keeps
   its lease answers `{ ok: true, skipped }` and the runner would count that a success, converting a
   failure into silence,
3. the dead-man's switch at `/api/health/jobs` uses a single `staleAfterSeconds: 1800`, while lease
   TTLs range from 5 to 15 minutes — so some suppressions are visible there and some are not, and
   nobody has checked which,
4. `run-jobs` exits non-zero on any failure, which Railway turns into a **"Deployment crashed"**
   email. That is currently the only thing that tells a human a job failed. It is real alerting
   wearing a misleading name.

**What would settle it.** A table of all thirteen jobs — duplicate cost, delay cost, lease TTL,
whether a suppressed run is detectable at the health endpoint — and one policy derived from it.
Then `jobs-lint` should enforce whichever shape is chosen, because the reason both exist is that
nothing checked.

## 2. Concurrency is proven by harnesses that do not run in CI, and I ran none of them today

`NOT CHECKED this session:` `claim-verify` · `engine-race` · `confirm-race` · `rls-verify` ·
`webhook-verify` · `stripe-mode-verify`.

**Why this matters more than it sounds.** The worst defect in this project's history — two guests
confirming the same hold and both getting the room — was invisible to ~1,800 unit tests, eleven
lints, and to `engine-race`, which was green throughout because it raced hold *creation* rather than
*confirmation*. When it was finally raced properly, **all twelve concurrent confirms won.**

**What I believe.** The guards are in place and were proven once. I have no evidence they still hold
after three weeks of changes, and "the tests pass" is not that evidence — it was not that evidence
the first time either.

**What would settle it.** Run all six against a scratch database, and then ask the harder question:
*what else has two halves where only one is raced?* `withTenantTransaction` is the obvious candidate
— every all-or-nothing write is supposed to use it and **nothing checks that they do.** That is a
lint somebody could write, and until it exists the rule is a convention.

## 3. RLS coverage is asserted by two different counts that nobody has reconciled

`rls-verify` reports 101/101 checks over 42 tables. `perimeter-lint` reports 77 protected tables.
Tables added since — `AppError`, `OperatorAlert`, `PlatformCredential`, `ProductTrial`,
`ClientAccount`/`Contact`/`Note` — carry `operator_only` or `tenant_isolation` policies as far as I
have read, but **I have not checked that the two lists agree, or that every new table appears in
both.**

**Confidence: low.** This is the kind of gap that is invisible until a hotel reads another hotel's
row, and the whole architecture rests on the database refusing that rather than the application
remembering to.

**What would settle it.** Make the two agree by construction: derive the expected set from the
Prisma schema and fail when a table has neither policy nor an explicit, stated exemption.

## 4. My own two fixes from today need a second reading

Both are small and both touch a surface somebody trusts.

**`isDeployMismatch`** (`packages/db/src/app-errors.ts`) drops Next's stale-deployment errors from
the error log. The tests are weighted toward proving it does *not* match. What I cannot prove is the
negative across the whole space of real messages: **is there any genuine fault whose message
contains `ChunkLoadError` or `Failed to fetch dynamically imported module` for a reason other than a
deploy?** I believe not — a chunk that 404s for any other reason is a broken build, which shows up
elsewhere — but that is reasoning, not evidence.

**`crossWiredFromRecord`'s `active` filter** (`packages/core/src/connectivity/cross-wired.ts`) is
the one I am least comfortable with, because I found its incompleteness only after shipping it.
`syncChannel` skips switched-off plans, so a cross-wire on one publishes nothing — but
`pushStopSellOverlay`, which pause and disconnect send, **deliberately does not filter on `active`**,
because closing a channel has to close everything live at the OTA. So a cross-wired mapping on a
switched-off plan is harmless right up until somebody pauses, and then it stop-sells the **wrong
room**. I corrected the stale-mapping alert to say this. What I did not do is make it visible in the
data.

**What would settle it.** Have `crossWiredFromRecord` return inactive cross-wires in a separate list
rather than dropping them, so each caller states its own case exactly: `act` for active ones, and a
stale-mapping alert that knows it is *also* cross-wired and says what a pause would do. One
detection, two readings — rather than one detection and a paragraph of prose asking people to
remember the exception.

## 5. Money is correct in parts, and has never been reconciled end to end by a person

The chain is: `computeStayCharges` → folio → `TaxInvoice` → VAT treatment → Stripe → refund. Each
link is tested. **The chain is not.**

Two specific doubts:

- **City tax inside the accommodation VAT base** (`f828cac`, 9 September) changed the arithmetic of
  every Bulgarian bill. It is tested against the reading of the law recorded in the specs. Nobody
  with a licence has confirmed that reading. That is item 2 in `ACTION-REQUIRED.md` and it is
  genuinely not an engineering question.
- **The all-in promise** — the first number a guest sees is the number they pay — is demonstrated on
  one booking (`RV-07NR0F`, €195, reconciled to €192 + €3). One case is an existence proof, not a
  property. An exhaustive test across occupancy, extras, multi-night, city-tax exemptions and
  currency would make it a property.

**What would settle it.** Take one real invoice end to end by hand, and separately write the
exhaustive reconciliation test. They answer different questions and both are worth having.

## 6. Nothing here has been measured above demo scale

Thirteen jobs run **sequentially** in one cron container, each with a 120-second timeout. Today
they take between 0.03 and 3.4 seconds. At fifty hotels, `auto-close-day` and `auto-assign` are the
ones that grow with the book, and `docs/CLOSE-DAY-LOAD-2026-09-09.md` measured transaction headroom
once, on demo data.

**What I believe.** It is fine now and I have no idea where it stops being fine.

**What would settle it.** Seed a scratch database at 50 and 200 properties and run the tick. The
question is not only "does it finish" but "does it finish inside the cron interval", because a tick
that overruns the next one is how leases start colliding for real rather than theoretically.

## 7. Multi-property behaviour is assumed, not verified

Hotel Sofia Group has three properties; every real client has one. Most screens resolve "the current
property" and I have not checked that the switcher behaves consistently across all 90 of them, or
that a job which iterates properties handles a tenant whose properties are in different time zones.

**`todayInTimeZone(property.timezone)`** is the rule, and `dates:lint` covers date *fields*. It does
not cover a report that sums across properties in two zones. **NOT CHECKED.**

## 8. The staff products in Bulgarian — large, and with a trap in it

The marketing site is bilingual; the three staff products and the guest booking page are English
only. This is on the roadmap as *The staff products in the hotel's own language*.

**The trap.** The founder's terminology rule is absolute — **канален мениджър**, never the
borrowing — and on the website it is enforced by `npm run terminology`. Across the products this is
thousands of strings, and a translation pass will reintroduce the banned phrase unless the same
check exists here first. **Write the lint before the first string is translated**, not after.

Second, smaller: the fonts now shipped locally carry `latin` and `latin-ext` only. Cyrillic is a
one-line addition to `scripts/fetch-fonts.mjs`, and it must happen in the same change or the first
Bulgarian screen renders in a fallback.

## 9. Smaller things I know about and have not done

- **`docs.reviosoft.app` has no HSTS and no frame protection.** It is a separate Railway service,
  deployed from neither repo, so nothing in `pnpm verify` can see it. I have not looked at how it is
  built.
- **`/design-lab` is still live and returns 200** on the marketing site, with `PhotoShowcase`,
  `ChromeWindow` and the `treatment` prop behind it. It is waiting on a decision, not on work.
- **A cleared publishable key cannot be expressed.** `planKeyEdit` makes an empty box mean *keep*,
  for every field, which removes the ambiguity that made the form unusable — and in exchange there
  is now no way to remove a stored publishable key short of replacing the whole credential. Nothing
  needs it today. If something does, it needs a deliberate control and not a blank box.
- **The `jobs` cron exits non-zero on any failure, including `demo-refresh`**, whose own comment
  says it is harmless if it fails. So a failed demo refresh sends the same "Deployment crashed"
  email as a failed night audit. Whether that is right is part of item 1.

## 10. What I would look at first, if it were me

In this order, and the order is the argument:

1. **Item 2** — run the six harnesses. It is an afternoon, it needs no decisions, and the one time
   this was skipped it hid the worst defect in the project.
2. **Item 1** — the retry policy. It is blocking a real improvement (a runner that retries) and it
   is cheap to decide once the table in that item exists.
3. **Item 4's second half** — the cross-wire/stop-sell exception, because it is a live customer's
   revenue and the fix is small and known.
4. **Item 3** — the RLS reconciliation, because it is the architecture's load-bearing claim.

Everything else can wait for a hotel to ask for it.
